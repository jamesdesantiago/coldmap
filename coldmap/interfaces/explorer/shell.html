What does coldmap do?
The data
Homicides that police reported to the FBI since 1976, and whether they identified who did it. Some agencies report incompletely, and coldmap shows where. The Murder Accountability Project (MAP), a nonprofit, publishes it.
What coldmap looks for
In each county it compares how often similar killings were solved, for example women killed by strangulation. It flags groups of years where they were solved far less often than expected.
Why that helps
Each police agency only sees its own cases. A run of similar unsolved killings across nearby towns or many years can go unnoticed. It may mean the cases are connected, or that an agency struggles to solve them. Either way, it deserves a closer look.
What you do with a flag
The explorer shows why each place was flagged, how often flags like it turn out to be false alarms, and known problems in the data. If one still looks real, a second person reviews it before it goes to MAP or the police tip line.

A flag is a pattern worth review, not evidence that cases are linked. Never contact families, witnesses or possible suspects. Read the ethics rules before sharing anything.

Nationwide

Every state searched in one pass and every flag ranked together, strongest first. Each place is still compared with the rest of its own state, as in a state build. Open any flag in its state's explorer for the chart and the case rows.

Plugins

Plugins add new ways to find patterns (detectors) or more context on each flag (enrichers). Add one, then switch it on or off below. States rebuild with your choice the next time you open them.
