    How to read this

    A case counts as solved if police had identified an offender when they filed the report. Cases solved later still show as unsolved, which is why known serial killings, such as William Suff's in Riverside County, California (1986–1991), still light up red. A flag is a pattern worth asking about, not evidence that cases are linked. Only investigators can link cases, through DNA, ballistics or other evidence.

    The 2021 correction: many 2021 reports (about 1,100 in California alone) list the offender as unknown but still name the relationship to the victim, sometimes as wife or girlfriend. When the correction is on, any case with a stated relationship counts as solved. Turn it off to see the raw file.

    Methods are grouped (all firearms together; beating includes blunt objects and hands; strangulation includes asphyxiation). "Not reported" cases are left out of flags. The case list covers women only, since that is where MAP focuses its serial-killer search. The data also has no names or addresses.

    Source: Murder Accountability Project Supplementary Homicide Report file, murders and non-negligent manslaughter only. Built with coldmap. Leads should go to MAP or the investigating agency, never to families or suspects.
